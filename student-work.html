Student Research & Scholarly Work

Research learning can take many forms

A meaningful research experience is not defined by a publication alone. It may be reflected in a careful question, a transparent analysis, a useful prototype, a well-supported synthesis, or a clear account of a study’s limitations.

Research Outputs Across Programs

Learners may develop research proposals, literature reviews, analytical reports, reproducible notebooks, technical demonstrations, research posters, presentations, and manuscript drafts.

The appropriate output depends on the program’s scope and the learner’s stage.

How Work Is Presented Here

Selected examples are shared with permission and with attention to student privacy, research ownership, and accurate attribution. Each published artifact identifies its program context, the learner’s contribution, and the status of the work.

A draft, submission, acceptance, presentation, and publication are different stages and are labeled accordingly.
