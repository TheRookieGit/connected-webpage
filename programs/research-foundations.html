Research Foundations

A practical foundation for asking better questions

Research Foundations introduces learners to the practices that make academic inquiry possible: finding credible scholarship, reading critically, connecting ideas, developing a research question, and communicating clearly.

An introductory format

It is designed for people beginning formal research or rebuilding the foundations for a new field.

One introductory format combines four live classes with approximately 10 hours of structured independent practice. Longer or school-integrated versions can revisit these skills with additional examples and feedback.

What learners practice

  • Finding and evaluating scholarly sources
  • Keeping useful reading notes
  • Distinguishing summary from synthesis
  • Identifying a feasible question
  • Linking questions to methods
  • Using citations accurately
  • Applying AI tools responsibly without replacing independent thought

Illustrative four-session sequence

01

Literature search and source evaluation

02

Critical reading and evidence synthesis

03

Research questions, design, and feasibility

04

Academic writing, citation, and responsible AI use

What Learners Can Develop

A search log, annotated reading record, literature matrix, and a short research concept note.

The purpose is to develop a research process learners can continue using, not to imply that a short introductory course produces a complete publishable study.

Where it can lead

Learners can move into a focused methods program or a mentored project when they have an appropriate foundation. Experienced learners may enter later stages directly.
