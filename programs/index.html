Programs

Research programs organized around how people learn

ConnectEd’s programs are organized by the kind of research experience they provide, not by a fixed list of disciplines. Learners may begin with foundations, develop a particular method, or enter a mentored project that matches their preparation. Schools and institutions can adapt these elements within a broader academic pathway.

Five program types

01 Foundations
01 Foundations

Research Foundations

An introduction to scholarly inquiry, reading, writing, research questions, and integrity.

It is designed for people beginning formal research or rebuilding the foundations for a new field.

Learn More: Research Foundations
02 Methods
02 Methods

Research Methods & Data Analysis

Focused learning in evidence synthesis, R or Python, statistical reasoning, and computational approaches.

Tool selection follows the question and learner preparation rather than a requirement to use every advanced technique.

Learn More: Research Methods & Data Analysis
03 Mentored Research
03 Mentored Research

Mentored Research Projects

Individual or small-group work on a scoped question with structured feedback and research communication.

The question, methods, available resources, and learner preparation determine what is feasible.

Learn More: Mentored Research Projects
04 Fellowships
04 Fellowships

Research Fellowships

Application-based research participation shaped by preparation, project fit, and host arrangements.

Eligibility, dates, participation costs, and any funding support are stated in the specific opportunity announcement.

Learn More: Research Fellowships

School & Institutional Programs

Research curriculum and enrichment developed around partner goals and educational requirements.

Collaboration may involve a course, a workshop sequence, a methods module, mentored projects, or a longer school-based research pathway.

Learn More: School & Institutional Programs

Program questions

Who are the programs for?

Our work spans secondary, undergraduate, graduate, and early-career stages. Each program has its own preparation requirements, and advanced topics may require additional background or a different pace.

Do I need a fully developed research idea?

Not always. Foundations and methods programs can help you develop the preparation for a project. Mentored research requires an appropriately scoped question, which is refined within the program’s learning process.

Are programs limited to the fields in the portfolio?

No. The portfolio presents representative examples. Other research areas may be considered when suitable expertise, resources, and a feasible educational scope are available.

Can I learn about the instructor before joining?

Relevant instructor or mentor information is shared through the program-planning process with appropriate permission. ConnectEd does not maintain a comprehensive public faculty roster.

Do the programs award university credit?

A general ConnectEd program listing does not indicate university credit. Any separately approved credit-bearing arrangement must identify the awarding institution and its specific requirements. Participation or completion documentation is not the same as academic credit.

Does a program guarantee publication or admission?

No general catalog listing guarantees publication, admission, a recommendation letter, an award, or an institutional appointment. Decisions by journals, conferences, universities, and individual recommenders are separate from participation in a learning program.

What do past offerings and illustrative outlines mean?

A past-offering label identifies a confirmed delivered program. An illustrative outline explains how the topic can be taught; it is not necessarily the exact syllabus used by a historical cohort.

Are community initiatives free?

Participation and funding arrangements vary by initiative and are provided in the relevant announcement. The 2025 PhD Incubator is described as a nonprofit initiative; future arrangements will be announced separately.

Looking for academic guidance beyond a course?

Our community initiatives include workshops, faculty-path conversations, and doctoral-preparation mentoring.
