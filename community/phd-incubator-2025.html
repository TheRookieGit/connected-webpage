PhD Incubator | 2025

Past initiative · September 2025 – January 2026 · Online

In 2025, ConnectEd organized its first nonprofit PhD Incubator to support people preparing to apply for doctoral study. A sustained mentoring initiative for the next stage of academic development.

From September 2025 through January 2026, more than twenty faculty members, postdoctoral researchers, and doctoral students worked with prospective applicants through a webinar series, small-group workshops, application-writing feedback, and mentor interviews.

The initiative grew out of ConnectEd’s wider community work. Its aim was to lower the communication and information barriers that often stand between strong applicants and the labs they hope to join.

Three formats of support

Application Webinars

A monthly series on the core stages of a PhD application, each led by one or two mentors. Sessions ran 60–90 minutes, combining a focused talk with Q&A, and some included breakout rooms, peer review, or practice exercises. Recordings remained available to participants.

Small-Group Workshops

Groups of up to five participants, organized by field and target country, met with a mentor during the application season. Participants submitted questions or drafts in advance and received live feedback on statements, strategy, and field-specific questions.

Mentor Interviews

Short recorded conversations with mentors on topics such as interviews, contacting prospective advisors, the value of a PhD, doctoral life in North America, and the skills each research area looks for. Mentors reviewed every interview before it was shared.

The webinar series

Eight sessions followed the application calendar, from early planning to final decisions. Sessions were held on Zoom with a capped audience so that everyone had time to interact with mentors, and priority went to incubator participants and ConnectEd volunteers. What participants received

  • Live access to the webinar series, with recordings available for replay.
  • Regular small-group sessions with mentors in their field during the application season.
  • Peer review and practice activities, including interview rehearsal.
  • Shared application resources, such as outreach-email and recommendation-letter templates, sample statements, and a bank of interview questions.
  • A discussion community with mentors and fellow applicants for questions throughout the cycle.

For mentors

The incubator was designed to fit around the schedules of working researchers. Its format offers a model for how faculty and doctoral students can take part in future editions.

How mentors took part

  • Each mentor chose two or three of the three formats.
  • Webinar speakers presented once, on the application topic they knew best.
  • Workshop hosts met their group about once a month during the application season, with no advance preparation required.
  • Interviews took 10–15 minutes online. Mentors could appear as an avatar, suggest edits, and approved the final cut.

What mentors received

  • An honorarium in recognition of their time, intended to support their own research.
  • For especially significant contributions, recognition as a ConnectEd Distinguished Mentor, which can be listed on a CV as academic service.
  • Connections with promising future scholars, and for faculty, a way to meet potential doctoral students.
  • Public engagement through interviews shared with tens of thousands of readers on ConnectEd’s social media channels.

Mentor with ConnectEd

Why a sustained format matters to our approach

The incubator was designed to give participants time to ask questions, reflect on their goals, and develop their application materials with feedback.

It complemented research training by focusing on the transition toward doctoral study and participation in academic communities.

Operating as a nonprofit

Participants paid a modest registration fee to keep the incubator sustainable. The fee went only to mentor honoraria and core operating costs, such as technology, event coordination, and administration. Outreach relied on low-cost channels, including our own social media and nonprofit partners.

Looking ahead
